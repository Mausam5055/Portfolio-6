Why it existed

Five Malayalis in Bangalore missed the food they grew up on. So did everyone they knew.

We are five working professionals from Kerala living in Bangalore. What reached us here came through supermarkets, online stores and ten minute apps: refined coconut oil, chilli powder with added colour, pickles that had waited months in a warehouse. The taste was drifting, and there was no way to know who had made any of it.

Quick commerce had made speed the thing every food brand competed on. Malayali Kada started as the opposite bet: that people would wait a week for food they could trace to a kitchen in Kerala.

Where it started

We never ran a survey. People kept asking for what we brought back from home.

It began with things we carried back for ourselves: my mother's pickles, jackfruit seeds from our own farmland, turmeric our neighbour grew, dried and powdered. Friends asked for the extra, then friends of friends. We set up stalls at society events and sold in small quantities. That became a WhatsApp community with a catalogue and weekend delivery, and only then a website.

What it doesn't

Every early buyer came through our own network, and nearly all were Malayali. The demand was real. How far it travels beyond that circle was unknown, and still is.

The mark

The first marks were symbols. The final one is a place.

Early routes reached for shorthand: an elephant, a monogram, a seal, over the descriptor "Authentic Kerala Groceries". The mark we kept is a Kerala gramam, an ordinary village with coconut palms and hills around a small home, under a rising sun. The descriptor moved from groceries to "The Artisan Kerala Store", because we curate, we don't stock shelves.

Decision 1

Every product page names a person and a place.

I wrote the Product Source tab as custom code on top of Shopify. It shows who prepared the item and pins where on a map. The anchovy pickle is Mr. Suneesh's, from Kottayam, and the Product Story tab tells how he makes it. A ten minute app shows a price and an arrival time. This page shows a kitchen.

Captured from the live store, 10 September 2026.

02
Decision 2

Delivery cost shows up on the home page, not as a surprise at checkout.

With the member's location, the store measures the distance to our nearest hub, Electronic City or Begur, and assigns a zone. The zone sets the free delivery threshold before a single item is in the cart. Two hubs let five people cover the city from where we already live.

The same widget captured from the live store at four Bangalore addresses. Each zone also changes the widget colour, from lime to grey.

03
Decision 3

Homechef food carries the cook's face and first name.

Freshly Made by Homechefs is our preorder program. Cakes, brownies and payasam are cooked only after an order comes in through the WhatsApp order window, then delivered the day they are made. I drew a sticker for each homechef, so the parcel itself says who cooked it: I made this freshly for you.

04
Decision 4

WhatsApp stayed the front door. The store is the shop behind it.

Members were on WhatsApp before the store existed, so I didn't try to move them. Product drops go out as designed posts that link to the store, with delivery days in every message. A second community, Kappa & Conversations, exists only for Malayalis in Bangalore to talk and meet.

Member counts and names are redacted from both screenshots.

05
Decision 5

Businesses meet us on the verandah, not through a sales form.

In a Kerala home, the verandah is where visitors are received before they come inside. The Verandah page is where brands, restaurants, PGs and apartment communities come to talk about partnerships, wholesale and festival supply. The store can grow into B2B without turning the home page into a pitch.

The illustration that closes The Verandah page on the live store.

Craft close up

One label system, from a 100 g pouch to a pickle jar with a personality.

Every pickle gets a name with attitude and its own flavour colour. The frame stays constant, "Organic & Homemade" on the band, the Malayalam name under the English one, so a shelf of eight still reads as one brand. Drag or use the arrows.

What I'd do differently

Photograph real batches from the first day. Many product photos on the store are still generated images. For a brand whose whole promise is that you can trace your food to a real kitchen, that is the one corner we shouldn't have cut. If I started again, a phone camera at every maker's house would come before the logo.
